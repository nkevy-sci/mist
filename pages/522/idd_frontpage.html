The Deliverable

IST522 Instructional Design taught its students how to conduct front-end analysis, an essential part of a designer’s work. An organization must first determine whether teaching and learning can help solve a problem or realize an opportunity. The faculty scaffolded this process by providing templates and outlines. My goal was to design a course on an instructional technique called concept mapping. My design document begins as follows.

A concept map is the visual display of declarative knowledge that both delays comprehension and organizes knowledge into a recognizable pattern by leveraging visual syntax (e.g. lines and shapes). Distinct concepts are related within propositions, defining the content of a concept map.

Reflection

I learned how to compose a design document with two main components, each comprising several subsections. First, I conducted front-end analysis to understand the environment in which I would be working. I read academic articles about the needs of undergraduate students, met with my faculty advisors, and connected those ideas to the university’s mission. I also read research about teaching concept mapping. Second, I began the design phase, selecting appropriate instructional strategies and techniques and adapting them to my context. Topic selection was challenging because I did not initially know which course would host my project. That decision would determine my target audience and influence the content of the learning materials. To address this uncertainty, I focused on the undergraduate student body as a whole and on the procedure students would need to learn regardless of the subject matter: how to make a concept map. The result is a detailed draft that explains the purpose of the intervention and describes a systematic learning experience for teaching that procedure.
