1.1, 1.2

Recognize a Concept Map

1.1 Given a graphic or diagram, graduate students will be able to determine if it is a concept map or not, with 80% accuracy.

1.2 Given a well-formed concept map, graduate students will be able to identify the component parts of a concept map, identifying 3 essential components and 1 variable component with 80% accuracy.

Learners will classify examples and non-examples. 12 true-false questions will be held in a Canvas quiz; 80% to pass.

Learners will select the answer that corresponds to an essential (or variable) component of a concept map. 7 multiple choice questions will be held in a Canvas quiz; 6/7 to pass.

The multiple choice questions will correspond to one or more specific concept map(s) that will be provided during this assessment. Elements will be labeled (e.g. a concept, a relationship, a proposition). Some questions will ask about the variable attributes like size and shape.

Behaviorist learning objectives (measurable and performance based).

Cognitivist multimedia principles (text and graphics).

Cognitivist generative processing theory (active learning).

Cognitive load theory (reduce extraneous processing by presenting only essential material).

Learners will...
Read a worked example.
Download a job-aid / hand-out (contains the properties of a concept map).
Take a low-stakes quiz.

Classify concepts (Merrill, 2025).
Worked examples.

SAM model should be used for rapid prototyping of the quiz. The reading and job-aid will be based on a literature review of concept mapping (including Novak & Gorwin, 1984).

Usability testing with eight participants.
Empathy Mapping will be used alongside the normal talk-aloud approach.

1.3, 1.4, 1.5

Make a concept map

1.3 Given a prompt, graduate students will be able to perform the procedure of concept generation, listing between 3 and 9 distinct concepts.

1.4 Given a list of propositions, graduate students will be able to convert the list of propositions into a concept map. Their map contains all of the information presented in the list of propositions.

1.5 Given a prompt, graduate students will be able to perform the skill of concept mapping, showing in 3 to 7 concepts and at least n-1 propositions (but not more than 3n-1) where n is the number of selected concepts. The design of their concept maps should match the design that was shown during the course.

Multiple choice questions will be held in a Canvas quiz.

1.3 Learners will be asked to select the concept that most strongly relates to a given statement. 2/3 to pass

1.4 Learners will identify the concept map that corresponds to a given proposition. 2/3 to pass

1.5 Learners will identify the errors in a concept map. This quiz will be framed as a scenario wherein the learner is grading a concept mapping according to a rubric (given). 2/3 to pass.

For example, The rubric asks for a map with 3 to 5 concepts. Select the best answer. Option 1. There are 5 concepts. Option 2. There are 6 concepts. Option 3. There are 4 concepts, and one of the relationships is incorrectly drawn like a concept.

Note: The instructor-less environment limits the assessment. The instructor cannot grade learner's maps.
