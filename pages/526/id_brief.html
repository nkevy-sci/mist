ID Brief

IST526

Making Concept Maps, Job Aid/Handout

Description

A mini-course, “Making Concept Maps,” will teach the skill of concept mapping to graduate students who attend California State University Monterey Bay. The learners, graduate students, will take this mini-course while attending Instructional Technology, a course required by their master’s program.

Problem Analysis

The master’s program in Instructional Science and Technology (MIST) at California State University Monterey Bay (CSUMB) does not teach its students how to make concept maps. During a required course, Interactive Media Design, concept maps are classified as organizational graphics (Clark & Mayer, 2023, Chap. 4). MIST would like to provide an evidence-based resource for its students to learn concept mapping, as it does with many instructional techniques. This, then, is the gap. There is no lesson on making concept maps, yet there could be.

There are two opportunities presented by this gap. First, future instructional designers could learn about concept mapping, later using concept maps to satisfy the need for visual media during an instructional design project. Second, MIST would like to know whether concept mapping can improve students’ initial learning of learning theory. Not all students take Theories of Learning (an established and required course) during their first semester. And discussions of learning theory are present in all courses. Ideally, students will be able to think critically about learning theories and explain how their work is informed by one or more theories of learning.

Description of Instructional Materials

Only one component of the project “Making Concept Maps” is described by this document. This document will be used to describe the instructional materials that will be developed during Interactive Media Design, a required course in the master’s program in Instructional Science at California State University Monterey Bay.

A job aid/handout will be provided to the learners for use during a subsequent activity. The job aid will be presented after the rationale and learning objectives. It will activate prior knowledge, provide a short explanation of concept mapping (essential attributes and variable attributes), and finally present a worked example. By worked example, I mean a graphic is shown with a question: Is it a concept map or not? The answer (and solution steps) are given.

Target Audience

Graduate students enrolled in IST524, Instructional Technology.

Learning Objectives (LO)

  • LO 1.1 Given a graphic or diagram, graduate students will be able to determine if it is a concept map or not, with 80% accuracy.
  • LO 1.2 Given a well-formed concept map, graduate students will be able to identify the component parts of a concept map, identifying 3 essential components and 1 variable component with 80% accuracy.

Assessment

LO 1.1 Learners will classify examples and non-examples. A Canvas quiz will contain 12 true-false questions. To pass, learners must correctly answer at least 80% of the questions.

Interestingly, this quiz (for LO 1.1) can double as a learning activity. In order to improve its instructional value, confirmation or corrections will be displayed as a learner submits each answer. This feedback will explain why examples or non-examples are classified as they are.

LO 1.2 Learners will select the answer that corresponds to an essential (or variable) component of a concept map. A Canvas quiz will contain 7 multiple-choice questions. Learners must correctly answer 6 out of 7 questions to pass.

The questions (assessing LO 1.2) will correspond to specific concept maps that will be provided in the question descriptions. Elements of these concept maps will be labeled (e.g., a concept, a relationship, a proposition). As well, some questions will ask about the variable attributes, like size and shape.

Instructional Strategies

To deliver instruction, an e-learning strategy will be implemented. The entire course design is founded upon Merrill’s First Principles and the Universal Model of Harold Stolovitch and Erica Keeps (Merrill, 2025; Stolovitch & Keeps, 2011). The learning objectives follow behaviorist conventions, being performance-based and measurable.

In particular, this activity and corresponding assessment are influenced by the designs of M. David Merrill. A strategy he called classify concepts prescribes the display of both examples and non-examples along with opportunities for learners to classify examples according to their attributes (Merrill, 2025). Also, cognitivist perspectives significantly influence the conception of this activity. Content design will be guided by the multimedia principles defined by Ruth Clark and Richard Mayer. These include but are not limited to the coherence principle, modality principle, and segmenting principle (Clark & Mayer, 2023).

Resources

The following resources will be used for design and implementation of the activity described by this document.

  • Articulate Rise 360, an e-learning authoring tool
  • Affinity Designer, drawing software
