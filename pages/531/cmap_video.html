CMap Video

IST531

Objective

Use a video as an instructional material. In other words, the content of the video will provide instruction about a particular topic. Conduct appropriate pre- and post-production, including the selection of instructional theories.

Video

Watch Phases of Concept Mapping on YouTube

Reflection

My final video provides instruction by revising the universal model of Harold Stolovitch and Erica Keeps (2011) according to both Merrill’s first principles and Gagné’s nine events of instruction. Merrill (2025) called for learning to be contextualized within a problem-solving activity. Here, the problem to be solved has to do with note-taking. Simply taking notes from a book just isn’t sufficient for meaningful learning.

The learner, the viewer of my video, has the opportunity to engage with a tell-show-ask strategy. After an introduction, the problem and learning objective are stated. Then the learner is reminded of what it is like to take notes. Afterward, concept mapping is demonstrated. There are two knowledge checks that ask learners to recall the phases of concept mapping. These questions function as simple recall practice.

Pre- and post-production went well. My video was produced using Adobe Premiere Pro. Transitions were selected from Premiere Pro’s default library. My graphics were drawn in Affinity Publisher. I used a fluid swivel-head tripod and a smartphone camera to shoot footage at my local public library. My audio was recorded using a USB microphone after much of the post-production was completed.

Script

The Scene

Scene direction: Show the actor in the library: an establishing shot. He is pulling a book off the shelf.

Hello, this is our friend Noah. He’s reading, learning something new.

Rationale or Problem

To improve learning, we often use both text and graphics. We don’t just use text.

We know that well-designed graphics complement text, improving learning (Clark & Mayer, 2023, Chap. 4).

Today we will learn about concept mapping, the kind of graphic organizer that identifies key ideas and shows how these ideas work together.

Scene direction: Sitting down with a book.

Learning Objective

Learners will be able to state the three phases of concept mapping.

Activate Prior Knowledge: Phase 1

Pause.

Read topic sentences. Ask yourself some questions. Read. Write keywords from headings and important details.

Scene direction: Show the actor sitting, then show page 1 of the text on the computer.

Phase 2

Pause.

Make a copy of your notes. Ask: What are these terms describing? What are the practical applications of what you’re learning? Is there more than one topic?

Scene direction: Show notes being reorganized on the computer.

Pause.

It’s time for a knowledge check.

Pause.

Knowledge Check: Practice

What is the first phase of concept mapping?

Demonstration (Tell-Show-Ask): Phase 3

To concept map, I use drawing software, in this case Google Drawings. Like writing, concept mapping requires revision. Think about the presentability of your map: Would someone else understand it? Does it look nice?

Pause.

Knowledge Check: Assessment

What are the phases of concept mapping?

Pause.

Hey, now you know more about concept maps.

Bye now.
