Capstone Project

IST699

Description

At California State University Monterey Bay (CSUMB), concept mapping was recognized as an underused instructional technique: the curriculum mentioned concept mapping during a course on multimedia design, but did not include a lesson on creating concept maps. To address this gap, I developed “Introducing Concept Maps,” a one-week, asynchronous micro-course, which would be delivered within a required course Instructional Technology. The module was implemented in Canvas LMS and included an e-learning activity authored in Articulate Rise 360, an online discussion forum, a pair of surveys, and a rubric. Students learned the essential elements of concept maps, practiced creating maps with generative artificial intelligence (GAI), and created maps to demonstrate their understanding of Behaviorism, Cognitivism and Constructivism. The design aimed to support meaningful learning through elaboration and recall while using GAI to scaffold students’ concept mapping abilities.

I conducted research on the students’ experiences with this module, focusing on two questions: “What are graduate students’ perceptions of using generative artificial intelligence (GAI) for concept mapping in a course on instructional technology?” and “How will graduate students describe their experience of creating size-constrained concept maps?” Students were required to complete the instructional activities, but participation in the research surveys was voluntary and no incentives were offered. I analyzed the collected data using a mixed approach combining open inductive coding with descriptive statistics. Presently, I'm organizing related codes chronologically to construct common narratives. In short, I'll be able to succinctly relate what students experienced during the learning experience. The institutional review board assigned exempt status to this research.
