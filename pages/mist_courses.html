IST520: Theories of Learning and Instruction

This course examines behavioral, cognitive, social, and biological theories of learning as foundations for instructional technology. Students connect learning strategies with research in cognitive science and neuroscience while developing an initial capstone idea.

Learning Outcomes

  • Articulate the relationships between theory, research, instructional and learning strategies and practice, consider the impact of technology on those relationships, and examine the relevance of newer and older approaches to training and learning.
  • Be introduced to new discoveries in neuroscience and cognitive science and examine the relevance of major learning theories in light of these discoveries.
  • Through individual and group effort, identify, compare and contrast, major learning theories and evaluate related instructional and learning strategies.
  • Through individual and group effort, students will analyze a learning object such as a course, tutorial or program, with respect to increasing the probability that the learning objectives will be met.
  • Groups and individuals will evaluate various learning theories and instructional and learning strategies related to learning situations.
  • Demonstrate an awareness of social and cultural influences on learning.
  • Examine emerging theories and strategies and their potential to advance learning, training and education through application of existing and future technological innovations.
  • Each individual will complete required human subjects research (CITI) training.
  • Using a provided rubric, each individual doing a Capstone project will develop an initial capstone idea.

IST522: Instructional Design

This course develops a systematic approach to planning instruction through individual and small-group work. Students conduct front-end analyses and create an instructional design blueprint that addresses instructional problems, content, implementation, and evaluation.

Learning Outcomes

  • Create a formal design blueprint (plan) for training or instruction.
  • Complete needs assessments, context, learner, and content analyses; design courses and lessons; and evaluate learning.
  • Define, exemplify, and apply key learning and instructional design concepts and procedures.
  • Evaluate and critique diverse instructional products and cases.
  • Apply relevant learning theories to an instructional problem.
  • Demonstrate professional collaboration and communication skills with team members.

IST524: Instructional Technology

This foundation course introduces the field and profession of instructional technology, including its history, current issues, and future trends. Students explore how learning theories, instructional design models, technologies, and professional practices work together.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify and apply basic terminologies with comprehension.
  • Identify and apply various instructional design models.
  • Identify and discuss tools, trends and issues in the field in their historical context.
  • Articulate differences between instructional technology, performance technology, and other related concepts.
  • Analyze and discuss pertinent case studies in application to chosen field.
  • Describe the essence of instructional theories and their impact on instructional design/technologies.
  • Describe and apply learning strategies, emphasis on technologies.
  • Demonstrate knowledge of the learner-centered paradigm.
  • Differentiate between learning theories and principles.
  • Demonstrate knowledge of Discovery Learning Model/Project-Based Learning.
  • Identify and apply e-learning best practices.
  • Differentiate types of alternative instruction (Reigeluth).
  • Analyze and compare Behaviorism, Constructivism and Cognitivism.
  • Conduct career planning, lifelong professional development plans.
  • Demonstrate knowledge of professional ethics.
  • Become familiar with professional organizations.
  • Evaluate and list several possible capstone ideas.

IST526: Interactive Multimedia

This course applies findings about mind and brain functioning and principles of media and information design to computer-based instruction. Students use screen capture and authoring software to build interactive learning modules enriched with multimedia.

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply appropriate learning theories and derived multimedia principles, solving an identified instructional problem, addressing a real need.
  • Apply the best practices of user interface design and interactive design.
  • Rapidly construct functioning, computer-based, interactive, multimedia learning experiences with appropriate feedback and assessment.
  • Select and use appropriate media, software, and production tools for specific needs.
  • Use help systems, online video, printed texts, web communities, and peer collaboration to quickly learn and apply new software and technologies.
  • Conduct formative evaluation.
  • Obtain and document user feedback.

IST541: Interactive Media

This course introduces HTML, CSS, and JavaScript as tools for creating interactive instructional media. Students explore online delivery platforms and design and build an interactive web-based instructional lesson.

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply HTML, CSS, and JavaScript as an interactive learning technology.
  • Use JavaScript to modify and control HTML content and style.
  • Create functions, conditions, and variables using JavaScript.
  • Adapt and modify existing code to incorporate into your projects.
  • Embed video and external resources on a web page using iframes.
  • Create an interactive instructional project that integrates the above technologies.

IST622: Assessment and Evaluation

This course examines how to judge education, training, and performance improvement programs against their intended objectives, desirability, and cost-effectiveness. Students use psychometric methodologies to assess learning and performance outcomes and evaluate curriculum design and instructional delivery.

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply basic statistics required for the field of instructional technology.
  • Design and develop appropriate instruments to assess learning process and outcomes.
  • Conduct appropriate usability, reliability, and effectiveness tests of instructional or performance interventions.
  • Design, conduct, analyze, interpret and report an evaluation project in the field of instructional and performance technology.

IST624: Research and Design

This course introduces quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods research and fundamental statistical concepts in instructional technology and education. Students synthesize literature and develop a comprehensive research proposal for a capstone project or another topic of interest.

Learning Outcomes

  • Demonstrate a clear understanding of qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods research methodologies.
  • Synthesize relevant literature to support research questions or hypotheses effectively.
  • Apply synthesized literature by developing a cohesive and well-structured literature review.
  • Evaluate and employ appropriate statistical techniques and methodological approaches.
  • Develop and apply knowledge of effective questionnaire design.
  • Exhibit a comprehensive mastery of course content by creating a complete research proposal, adhering to all aspects of APA formatting and standards.

IST626: Advanced Instructional Design

This course applies instructional design to real-world projects while examining how design decisions influence learners and organizations. Students integrate evidence-based design, project management, change management, and design thinking while collaborating with clients to develop and evaluate instructional solutions.

Learning Outcomes

  • Use the instructional design process to plan, develop, and evaluate learning transfer and solutions that solve real-world problems using design thinking practices and evidence-based design principles and strategies.
  • Demonstrate professional collaboration and communication skills with clients, class team members, and the instructor.
  • Identify organizational needs as they relate to performance improvement.
  • Create a design blueprint (plan or storyboard) for training or instruction.
  • Incorporate design thinking principles and change management strategies when appropriate.
  • Manage an instructional design project from beginning to end.
  • Demonstrate the use of a project management system for tracking project progress.

IST699: Graduate Capstone

This course guides the planning, development, evaluation, and presentation of a substantial project in instructional science and technology. Students integrate real-world learning and performance issues with advanced information technology in a project shaped by their interests, expertise, and educational and career goals.

Learning Outcomes

  • Capstone Project - providing an original application of technical/professional knowledge in the field of instructional science and technology.
  • Written communication at the professional level.
  • Oral communication at the professional level.
  • Overall knowledge/skills developed in the MIST curriculum are demonstrated via an online portfolio.
  • Research skills such as data collection, data analysis, reporting, etc.
  • Career Planning.
